# AuroraWeave — customer discovery brief

## Purpose

AuroraWeave is an early-stage hypothesis for a woven thermal-protection-material architecture intended for high-temperature, reusable aerospace applications. It is not yet a qualified product, and no performance claims should be treated as established until independently tested.

## The customer problem

Reusable launch and re-entry vehicles need thermal-protection systems that balance high-temperature capability, mass, durability, manufacturability, repairability, and turnaround cost. NASA identifies woven TPS as a real research direction because fibre placement and composition can be tailored to mission conditions, but material systems require characterisation and qualification testing before flight use.

## The hypothesis to evaluate

A continuously woven, Möbius-inspired architecture may offer a useful route to managing edge effects, load redistribution, attachment interfaces, or thermal cycling in a material system. The specific mechanism, material stack, and operating envelope remain to be defined and tested.

## What we are asking for

We are seeking a short technical discovery conversation with a materials, TPS, hypersonics, or advanced-manufacturing lead to answer four questions:

1. Which mission segment and heating regime has an unmet need that a woven reusable TPS could credibly address?
2. What material architecture, coupon dimensions, test conditions, and baseline would make an initial evaluation worthwhile?
3. Which metrics would be decisive: backside temperature, recession, oxidation, retained strength, cycle durability, attachment performance, manufacturability, or cost?
4. Which independent lab or facility is appropriate for the first screening test?

## Evidence we will earn before making commercial claims

- Exact fibre, matrix, coating, weave geometry, thickness, density, and attachment definition.
- A controlled baseline comparison.
- Raw results for thermal exposure, mass loss/recession, oxidation, mechanical retention, and repeat-cycle degradation.
- Independent technical review, followed by accredited high-enthalpy or arc-jet testing only if early results justify it.

## What we will not claim

We will not claim a solved heat-shield problem, a particular temperature limit, reuse count, cost, flight readiness, or performance advantage before reproducible evidence supports it.
